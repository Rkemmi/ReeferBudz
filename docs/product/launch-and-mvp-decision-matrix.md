# ReeferBudz Launch and MVP Decision Matrix

Status: Final
Date: August 7, 2026

## 1. Purpose

This document defines what belongs in the first launch version of ReeferBudz, what belongs after launch, and what must remain blocked until required product, safety, privacy, or operational rules are complete.

Its purpose is to prevent the product from expanding through impulse, feature pressure, or unfinished ideas that weaken clarity.

## 2. Decision Categories

Every major ReeferBudz page or feature must be placed in one of four categories:

### A. Launch Now

This item is required for the first stable public and member release.

### B. Launch Later

This item belongs to the product, but it is not required for the first stable release.

### C. Blocked Until Dependencies Are Complete

This item may belong in the product, but it must not launch until the required safety, privacy, moderation, data, or product rules are fully defined and supported.

### D. Not in MVP

This item is non-core, distracting, optional, or outside the first launch goal.

## 3. MVP Launch Standard

A feature belongs in MVP only if it supports the first stable version of ReeferBudz as:

- an adults-only friendship and community platform
- a product with a clear website-to-app path
- a product with brand clarity
- a product with basic privacy and safety structure
- a product with a usable signed-in member experience
- a product that does not overextend beyond its current protections

If a feature adds complexity faster than trust, clarity, privacy, or safety can support it, it should not be in MVP.

## 8. Not in MVP by Default

These items should be treated as non-MVP unless a later decision deliberately changes their status:

- shop as a major product surface
- advanced social gamification
- real-time activity systems
- expanded social-networking layers beyond the core discovery experience
- any feature that introduces higher-risk member-to-member interaction before reporting, blocking, and moderation are fully operational

## 9. MVP Gate Rules

A feature should not enter MVP unless:

- it has a clear product purpose
- it has a defined place in the website/app structure
- it supports the friendship-first platform identity
- it does not overclaim trust or safety
- it does not require missing safety or data rules to function responsibly

A feature should stay out of MVP if:

- it introduces high-risk interaction too early
- it creates dating-app confusion
- it creates cannabis-commerce confusion
- it requires storage, moderation, or privacy rules that are not yet finalized
- it adds noise without proving the core product value

## 10. Launch Logic

The first stable launch of ReeferBudz should prove:

- the public brand and product are clear
- adults can enter the platform through the correct path
- members can build a profile and control basic settings
- members can discover compatible Budz in a privacy-conscious way
- the product feels coherent across website and app
- the product does not promise more than its safety and account foundation can support

## 11. Launch / MVP Do's

ReeferBudz should:

- launch the clearest version of the core product first
- protect the MVP from feature sprawl
- use blocked status when dependencies are real
- make launch decisions based on product integrity, not novelty
- keep higher-risk social features behind clear safety prerequisites

## 12. Launch / MVP Don'ts

ReeferBudz must not:

- treat every good idea as a launch feature
- launch risky interaction tools before support systems exist
- let merch or side concepts distract from the core product
- confuse interesting with necessary
- overstuff MVP until the product loses clarity

## 13. Short Launch Statement

The first ReeferBudz launch should include the public trust-and-entry website plus the core member experience of onboarding, profile, settings, and discovery, while holding back higher-risk or higher-complexity features until the required rules and protections are fully in place.
